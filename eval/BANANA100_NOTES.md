# Banana100: what is actually in the dataset

Inspected with `python -m eval.banana100 inspect` on 2026-10-12, before writing any runner. Nothing
here is assumed from the paper; it is what the files contain.

Dataset: [`kenantang/Banana100`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/kenantang/Banana100) on Hugging Face,
licence **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**. Paper: *Banana100* (arXiv 2604.03400).

## Layout

| Path | Content |
| --- | --- |
| `banana100.parquet` | One file, **218 GB**. 28,000 rows in 14 row groups of 2,000 rows. |
| `inputs/banana/*.png` | 11 seed images generated by Nano Banana Pro (5632 × 3072 and similar). |
| `inputs/spice/*.png` | 2 seed images generated by SPICE. |
| `hyperparameters/*.jsonl` | 20 chain definitions, 100 lines each: `prompt`, `resolution`, `seed`, `temperature`. |
| `metrics/*.csv` | The authors' no-reference IQA scores. Not used here. |
| `scripts/run.py` | The generation loop. Each step sends the previous output and the prompt to `gemini-3-pro-image-preview`. |

Parquet columns: `model`, `image_name`, `hyperparameter`, `run_idx`, `step`, `output_image` (PNG
bytes), `reasoning_trace`. A row is one step of one chain. Each row group holds 4 chains × 5 runs ×
100 steps, in order, for one or two seed images.

## Chains that matter for Peel

| Chain | Prompt at every step | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `replica`, `replica_1K`, … | "Produce an exact replica of the provided image, with no alterations." | Pure drift: nothing is requested. |
| `add_apple` | "Add an apple on the table" | A local edit repeated 100 times. |
| `add_apple_denoise` | "Add an apple on the table, and then remove the noise in this image while keep other elements in this image unchanged." | Same, with an explicit request to preserve the rest. |
| `add_100_fruits` | "Add an apple on the table", "Add a banana on the table", … | 100 different local edits. |

## Why we only use the first chain of a row group

The `output_image` column is dictionary-encoded and Snappy-compressed. The first ~2 GB of images of
every row group sit in **one dictionary page**. pyarrow has to download and hold that entire page to
return a single row; on an 8 GB laptop the process was killed after 11 GB of transfer.

`eval/banana100.py` reads the page by hand instead: it range-requests the start of the dictionary
page, parses the Thrift `PageHeader`, and decodes the raw Snappy stream only as far as the first N
images (PNG bytes are incompressible, so the stream is almost all 64 kB literals and a pure-Python
decoder is fast). That yields the leading steps of the **first chain of a row group** for about
5 MB per step, and nothing else cheaply. Images deeper in a row group are in plain pages of 1,024
values (about 7 GB each) and are out of reach on this machine.

Row groups and their leading chain:

| Group | Leading chain | Fetched |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | `banana/fog/replica` run 1 | steps 1–20 |
| 7 | `banana/library/replica` run 1 | steps 1–20 |
| 8 | `banana/peacock/replica` run 1 | steps 1–20 |
| 9 | `banana/sand/replica` run 1 | steps 1–20 |
| 10 | `banana/table/add_apple_denoise` run 1 | steps 1–20 |

So the chain evaluation covers one local-edit chain and four replica chains, 20 steps each. That is
a small sample and it was chosen by what was reachable, not at random; `docs/eval.md` says so too.

## Other observations

- Outputs are 2816 × 1504 for a 5632 × 3072 input: a different size **and** a slightly different
  aspect ratio (1.872 vs 1.833), so every step also reframes the picture a little.
- We store fetched steps at a long side of 1280 px and evaluate at 1024 px.
- Fetched images are cached under `eval/cache/` and are not committed. Two reduced-size pairs and
  one reduced-size chain are bundled as examples, with attribution, under the dataset's licence.
